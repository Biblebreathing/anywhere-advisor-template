# Fact Sheet — Retirement Paycheck · Colorado · 2026

Every number cites a fact ID from `fact-registry.md`. Educational only.

## 1. The core problem, in one paragraph
Greg's engineers saved for 30 years in accounts built to grow. Now they need $200K–$300K a year that lasts for both spouses (KB §3), possibly for a long time: at 65 the average American has about 20 more years (women 20.8, men 18.4), and half live longer than average [F-055]. Retiring isn't always on their schedule. In the 2026 Retirement Confidence Survey, retirees' median retirement age was 62 and nearly half retired earlier than planned [F-066]. The first years are the most dangerous for withdrawals. Bengen's 1994 historical study found that retirements beginning just before the 1973–74 bear market did the most damage, and he advised caution "during the early years of retirement" [F-061]. 2022 was a fresh reminder: the S&P 500 fell 19.4% for the year and 25.4% from peak to trough [F-062, F-063]. Social Security timing is a lever worth tens of percent: claiming at 62 cuts the benefit 30%, and waiting to 70 adds 24% over the age-67 amount [F-057, F-058].

## 4. Myths vs. facts
| What people believe | What's actually true | Fact ID |
|---|---|---|
| "I only need to plan to my mid-80s." | That's roughly the *average*. At 65, the average woman has 20.8 more years and the average man 18.4, so a couple should plan for one spouse living well beyond that. | F-055 |
| "I'll work until 65 or 67." | Retirees' median retirement age is 62, and nearly half retired earlier than planned. | F-066 |
| "A bad year doesn't matter if the average return is fine." | With withdrawals, *order* matters. In Bengen's historical tests, the worst outcomes came from retiring just before a major decline. | F-061 |
| "Taking Social Security at 62 and investing it is always better." | Claiming at 62 permanently cuts the benefit by 30% (FRA 67); each year of delay to 70 adds 8%. The trade-off depends on longevity, a surviving spouse's benefit, and taxes. | F-057, F-058 |
| "If I claim early and keep working, I keep everything." | Before FRA, $1 is withheld for every $2 earned above $24,480 (2026). | F-060 |
| "The market drop in 2022 wasn't that bad." | The S&P 500 fell 19.4% for the year (−18.1% with dividends) and 25.4% from its January peak to its October low. | F-062, F-063 |
