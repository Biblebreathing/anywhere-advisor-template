# Fact Sheet — Tax ("The Tax Trap") · Colorado · 2026

Every number cites a fact ID from `fact-registry.md`. Educational context, not tax advice. Have Greg's EA/CPA (Fort Collins Tax Advisory Group) review every example before it ships.

## 1. The core problem, in one paragraph
Greg's ideal client, an engineer or technical professional of 60 to 68 with $1M–$4M in pre-tax IRAs and 401(k)s, has a tax bill built into those accounts. Every dollar out is ordinary income [F-002]. Required withdrawals start at 73, or at **75 for anyone born in 1960 or later**, which covers most of Greg's 60–66-year-old prospects [F-013, F-014]. Those withdrawals then raise the share of Social Security that is taxed (up to 85%) [F-019], and they can trigger Medicare IRMAA surcharges based on income from two years earlier [F-021, F-022, F-024]. Add RSUs, which are taxed as wages when shares are delivered [F-025], and rental property carrying depreciation recapture [F-027, F-030], and the "tax trap" is real. The window between retiring and starting RMDs is when Roth conversions, QCD planning (from 70½) [F-017], and the timing of sales can be modeled on purpose.

## 4. Myths vs. facts
| What people believe | What's actually true | Fact ID |
|---|---|---|
| "My RMDs start at 72." | 73 today, and 75 for people born in 1960 or later. Many of Greg's prospects have more years before RMDs than they think, which means a longer window for planned conversions. | F-013, F-014 |
| "Roth conversions only make sense if I'm under the Roth income limit." | The income limits apply to Roth *contributions*. Conversions have no income cap, but the converted amount is taxable income that year and can't be undone. | F-011, F-016 |
| "The new senior deduction made Social Security tax-free." | The $6,000 deduction (2025–2028) lowers taxable income, but it phases out above $75K/$150K MAGI and doesn't change how Social Security is taxed. Up to 85% of benefits can still be taxable. | F-005, F-019 |
| "Medicare costs the same for everyone." | Above $218K MAGI (joint), Part B rises from $202.90 to as much as $689.90 a month per person, plus Part D surcharges, based on income from two years earlier. A big conversion or property sale at 63 can show up in premiums at 65. | F-021, F-022, F-024 |
| "My RSUs are taxed when I sell." | RSU shares are compensation income when they're delivered (usually at vesting), at fair market value. Only the change in value after that is capital gain or loss. | F-025 |
| "A 1031 exchange lets me retire from being a landlord tax-free." | A 1031 only defers tax, only for real property, and only on strict 45/180-day deadlines. You still own real estate afterward. | F-028, F-029 |
| "An installment sale defers all my tax." | Depreciation recapture is taxed in full in the year of sale, even if most of the cash comes later. | F-030 |
| "Colorado taxes my retirement income like wages." | At 65+, all taxable Social Security plus up to $24,000 per person of pension/IRA income can be subtracted. | F-032, F-034 |

## 6. Recent and upcoming changes
- **One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025):** new $6,000 senior deduction for 2025–2028 [F-005]; $15M estate exemption from 2026, indexed after [F-038]. 2026 brackets and standard deduction reflect OBBB amendments [F-001].
- **SECURE 2.0:** RMD age 73 now and 75 for those reaching 74 after 2032 [F-014]; QCD limit indexed ($111,000 in 2026) [F-018]; age 60–63 "super catch-up" ($11,250) and the Roth-catch-up wage threshold ($150,000) [F-010].
- **Senior deduction sunsets after 2028** [F-005]. That makes 2025–2028 conversion modeling time-sensitive `[INFERRED]`.
- **Colorado:** possible TABOR-triggered temporary rate cuts through 2035 (2026 rate unconfirmed) [F-031]; HB 26-1062 failed [F-037].
- **Medicare:** 2027 IRMAA brackets will be based on 2025 MAGI [F-024]; 2027 figures not yet published as of the access date.
