## 2. The Through Line

> **Wrong belief:** "We're not rich, and we have a will (or we'll get one), so we're fine."
> **True belief:** "An estate plan isn't about how much you have or what you'll owe. It's about who decides when you can't, and keeping your family out of court."

**Why the research supports this angle:** Colorado has no estate or inheritance tax [F-040], and the federal exemption is $15,000,000 per person for 2026 [F-038], so almost none of Greg's $1M-$4M households face a death tax. What they do face is court time and incapacity. Colorado's small-estate affidavit covers only personal property under $88,000 (2026) [F-042], while the median Larimer County home is worth $610,000 [F-076] and 82.1% of householders 65+ own their home [F-077]. Probate has statutory minimums: the will is lodged within 10 days [F-047], nothing can be filed before 120 hours [F-043], creditors get at least 4 months [F-044], and an informal closing can't come sooner than 6 months after appointment [F-046]. Powers of attorney made since 2010 are durable by default [F-051], but someone still has to sign one.

**The turn line (slide 6):** "It's not about what you'll owe. It's about who decides."

## 9. Seeded questions (with prepared answers, under one minute each)

1. **"Do I need a trust, or is a will enough?"** "That depends on your family, and it's an attorney's call, not mine. Here's how to think about it: a will tells the court what to do. A funded trust, beneficiary forms, and a Colorado beneficiary deed are the roads that can keep assets out of court. The question to bring your attorney is, which road should each of our assets be on?"
2. **"What does 'funding a trust' mean?"** "It means retitling assets into the trust's name, or pointing beneficiary forms at it, so the trust actually controls them. A trust only controls what's titled in it. That's the plumbing we talked about. In a PATH Review, I check the accounts against what your documents say."
3. **"What if I own property in another state?"** "Good question, and an important one for your attorney, because each state has its own process for real estate located there. Write it on page six so we make sure it gets asked."
4. **"How often should I update my plan?"** "Look at it after every life event: a marriage, a divorce, a death, a birth, a move, a new account. And walk the whole PATH once a year. Beneficiary forms are the piece that drifts most often."
5. **"What does this cost, and who drafts the documents?"** "The PATH Review is free. Documents are drafted by a licensed attorney, and the attorney sets their own fees. Here's how my side works: advisors are paid to fund trusts, never to sell or create them." `[COMPLIANCE: Greg's approver must confirm this answer and how the firm's current document offer is described.]`

**Rule for every live answer:** Never interpret a specific person's document. Say: "That's a great question for your attorney, and I'd be glad to look at how it lines up with your accounts in a PATH Review."
