## Page 2: Big Idea 1. A will doesn't keep your family out of court *(slide 20)*

**In one sentence:** A will is the instruction manual for the court. Beneficiary forms, a Colorado beneficiary deed, and a funded trust are the roads around it.

Next to each asset, write its road today: **W** (will only), **B** (beneficiary form), **D** (beneficiary deed), **T** (titled in a funded trust), or **?** (don't know).

**Colorado facts to remember** (general education; confirm with your attorney):
- Colorado has no estate tax or inheritance tax, and the federal estate tax exemption is $15 million per person for 2026.
- The small-estate affidavit covers personal property under $88,000 (2026 deaths). It generally does not cover real estate. The median Larimer County home is worth $610,000.
- A will must be filed with the district court within 10 days of death.

## Page 3: Big Idea 2. The deadline isn't death. It's the day you can't decide *(slide 23)*

**In one sentence:** Being married doesn't automatically let your spouse sign for you. A durable financial power of attorney does.

| | Me | My spouse |

Good to know: In Colorado, a power of attorney made on or after January 1, 2010 is durable by default (it keeps working if you become incapacitated) unless it says otherwise.

## Page 4: Big Idea 3. A plan nobody maintains is a plan that fails *(slide 26)*

**In one sentence:** Documents don't update themselves, so somebody has to keep the pieces connected.

☐ **Trust funding.** We have a trust, and we've opened accounts, refinanced, or bought property since it was signed.
☐ **Special needs.** Someone we'd leave money to receives, or may someday receive, needs-based benefits.
☐ **Blended family.** One or both of us has children from an earlier relationship.
