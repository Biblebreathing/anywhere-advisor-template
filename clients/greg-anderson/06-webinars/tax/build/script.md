## Slide 26: What You Can Time in the Window
*Act 2 · cards · angle: method*

"Workbook, page four. Four things you can time."

"One. Roth conversions. You move money from a pre-tax IRA to a Roth, and the amount is taxed as ordinary income that year. You can't undo it. The Roth income limits apply to contributions, not conversions. And each conversion starts its own five-year clock for a penalty rule. The question is whether paying some tax now, at a rate you can see, beats paying it later at a rate you can't."

"Two. RSUs. The IRS lets employers withhold a flat twenty-two percent on supplemental pay. Check whether that's what you see on your vests. Twenty-two percent withheld isn't your tax. In 2026, a married couple's twenty-four percent bracket starts at two hundred eleven thousand four hundred dollars, and thirty-two percent at four hundred three thousand five hundred fifty. If your vests land in those brackets, the difference shows up in April."

"Three. Medicare usually starts at sixty-five. Because of the two-year look-back, your income the year you turn sixty-three can set that premium. You can appeal after a life-changing event, like retiring. Better to plan it than appeal it."

"Four. For Colorado folks, the state return changes at sixty-five. From then on, all your taxable Social Security can be subtracted, plus up to twenty-four thousand dollars a year per person of pension and IRA income. From fifty-five to sixty-four, it's up to twenty thousand, and many of you will be over the income test for the full Social Security break.
