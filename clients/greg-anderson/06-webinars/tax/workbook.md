## Page 2: Big Idea 1 · Not All Dollars Are Equal

**The mix matters more than the total.**

| Bucket | How it's taxed when it comes out (2026 rules) |
|---|---|
| **Pre-tax** | Every dollar out is ordinary income in the year you take it. Required withdrawals start at 73 or 75. <!-- F-002, F-013, F-014, F-016 --> |
| **Roth** | No required withdrawals during your lifetime. Under current law, a *qualified* withdrawal isn't counted as income. Qualified means **both**: (1) you're 59½ or older (or another qualifying event), **and** (2) your first Roth IRA contribution was at least five tax years ago. <!-- F-015, F-102 --> |
| **Taxable** | Gains on investments held more than a year get long-term rates: 0% on taxable income up to $98,900 (married filing jointly), then 15%, then 20% at the top. <!-- F-006, F-007 --> |
| **RSUs** | Taxed as wages when shares are delivered to you, usually at vesting. Only the change in value after that is a capital gain or loss. <!-- F-025 --> |

## Page 4: Big Idea 3 · The Window

**You can't control Congress. You can control timing.** Circle the ones that apply to you.

1. **Roth conversions.** Taxed as ordinary income in the year converted. Can't be undone. No income cap (the Roth income limits apply to contributions). Each conversion has its own five-year clock for the 10% additional tax. <!-- F-016, F-011, F-102 -->
2. **RSU vests and sales.** Employers may withhold a flat 22% on supplemental pay. In 2026, a married couple's 24% bracket starts at $211,400 of taxable income and the 32% bracket at $403,550. Withholding isn't your final tax. <!-- F-103, F-002, F-025 -->
3. **The Medicare look-back.** Income in the year you turn 63 can set your premium at 65. <!-- F-024, F-106 -->
4. **Colorado at 65** *(Colorado residents)*. At 65+, all taxable Social Security can be subtracted on the state return, plus up to $24,000 per person of pension and IRA income. At 55 to 64, up to $20,000, and the full Social Security subtraction applies only if AGI is $95,000 or less (joint) / $75,000 (single). <!-- F-032, F-034, F-035 -->
5. **QCDs from 70½.** Give directly from an IRA to a qualified charity, up to $111,000 per person in 2026; it can count toward your RMD. <!-- F-017, F-018 -->
6. **The senior deduction.** An extra $6,000 per person 65+ for 2025 through 2028, phasing out above $75,000 single / $150,000 joint (modified AGI). <!-- F-005 -->
