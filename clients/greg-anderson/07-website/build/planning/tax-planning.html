Most people assume taxes go down in retirement. For good savers, they often don't.

Required withdrawals, Social Security taxation and Medicare surcharges can stack up in the same years. Planning the years before that is where the opportunity is.

Does this sound familiar?

  • Most of your savings is pre-tax
  • You've heard about Roth conversions but no one has run your numbers
  • Your tax preparer and your advisor never talk
  • You have restricted stock and you're not sure when to sell

What every family should know

1. Required withdrawals start later now, and get bigger

Required minimum distributions now start at 73, or 75 for anyone born in 1960 or later, so balances can keep growing pre-tax until then.

2. Medicare looks back two years

Medicare Part B and D surcharges are based on your income from two years earlier, so a big conversion or sale this year can raise your premiums later.

3. Your return is the map

Our tax team starts with your actual return and runs "what if" scenarios: conversions, withdrawals, RSU sales, rental or business sales.

Questions people ask

Should I do a Roth conversion?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. It depends on your bracket now and later, your Medicare premiums and your heirs. That's what the CPA What-If review is for.
