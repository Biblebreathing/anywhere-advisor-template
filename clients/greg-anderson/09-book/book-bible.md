## Step 1: Big Idea Brief
- **Core problem:** Good savers reach retirement with money built to grow, not to pay them, and with tax, legal and investment decisions handled by three people who never talk. The result is "head trash": a vague sense that there is no plan.
- **Unique solution:** The PATH Plan puts Paycheck, Assets, Taxes and Heirs on one table, in that order, and walks it again every year.
- **Transformation promise (compliant):** A written plan you can explain to your spouse: where the paycheck comes from, how it's positioned, how the taxes are planned, and how your family is protected. "A path to run on." Not a promise of returns or tax results.
- **Hooks:**
  - Problem: "Your savings were built to grow. Now they have to pay you."
  - Solution: "Four letters, one table: Paycheck, Assets, Taxes, Heirs."
  - Authority: "A tax law ended my first career before it started. I've planned around tax changes ever since."
- **Market position (Stage 03 competitor scan):** Local competitors teach estate workshops (Estate Planners Group), podcasts (Saulnier) or investment-led planning (Mercer, which also targets engineers and RSU holders). James Lange owns "Roth conversion" nationally. This book's angle: **the coordination of all four, for numbers-first readers, with rentals and businesses treated as part of the plan.** Never "the only office that does this."
- **Risk reversal (appointment only):** no cost, no obligation, you keep your PATH Map. No outcome guarantees.

**Hell journal (Linda, ~300 words):**
> Dave said he wants to retire next June. He says it like it's settled, and maybe the numbers are, in his spreadsheet. I don't understand the spreadsheet. I don't think he does either, if I'm honest, because every time I ask how much we can spend he says "it depends." Depends on what? The market? We watched it drop in 2022 and he didn't sleep for a week. My sister's husband died two years ago and she spent eight months with lawyers and the bank, and half the accounts had his first wife's name on them. Our will is from when the kids were in grade school. I don't even know where it is. Dave says the 401(k) is "ours," but our neighbor told me the government gets a third of it when we take it out. Is that true? Nobody tells you. The CPA does our return in March and we don't hear from him again. The investment guy calls when he has something to sell. And the duplex. The tenant called twice this month about the water heater. Dave wants to keep it "for income," but I'm the one who answers the phone. I lie awake doing math I'm not good at. What if he's gone first? What would I even do on the Monday after?

**Heaven journal (Linda, ~300 words):**
> We have a one-page map now. It's on the fridge, which Dave thinks is funny. It shows what comes in each month next year, and the year after, and what happens if one of us is gone. Our Social Security dates are decided; Dave waits, I don't, and I understand why. The money we'll live on for the next few years isn't sitting in the same place as the money we won't touch for fifteen, so when the market dropped last spring I noticed, and I didn't panic. The tax people looked at our actual return and showed us a few years where converting some of the 401(k) makes sense, before the required withdrawals start. We have a trust now, and powers of attorney for both of us, and every beneficiary form matches. There's a binder, and the same documents are online, and our son knows where both are. We sold the duplex last fall with a plan for the tax, and that money is part of the paycheck now. No more water heaters. Once a year we sit down at one table, the tax side and the investment side together, and walk through it again. Dave still has his spreadsheet. He just doesn't need it to sleep.

## Step 5: 8-Point Story Arc
1. **Stasis:** Good savers on autopilot; the 401(k) grows; three advisors in three offices.
2. **Trigger:** A retirement date gets set (or a friend's death, a market drop, an RSU vest, a tenant call).
3. **Quest:** They try to figure out "how much can we spend" with spreadsheets, seminars and online calculators.
4. **Surprise:** The problem isn't the size of the number. It's that the money was built to grow, not to pay them, and nobody is coordinating taxes and documents. (The Three-Office Problem.)
5. **Critical choice:** Keep guessing ("we'll deal with it later"), or put all four pieces on one table.
6. **Climax:** The PATH Review: Paycheck, Assets, Taxes, Heirs, in order.
7. **Reversal:** The surviving-spouse question gets answered, the tax years get planned, the documents get signed. (Story 2's lesson: "one year and one day.")
8. **Resolution (CTA seed):** A path to run on, walked again every year. Start with your PATH Review, or do these steps with any advisor who'll sit at one table with your CPA and attorney.
