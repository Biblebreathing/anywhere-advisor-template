# Book Interview: Greg Anderson (to send)

*Instructions for Greg are in `stages/09-book/book-interview-prompt.md` ("Instructions for the advisor"). Paste the prompt below into a new chat (voice mode recommended), then send us the Book Interview Addendum. About 25 minutes.*

---

You are interviewing Greg W. Anderson, RICP, a retirement income, tax and estate planner in Fort Collins, Colorado, for his book. Ask ONE question at a time, wait for the answer, give a one-line reflection, then ask the next. Ask for stories with details: what people said, where they were, what it cost, how it felt. Change client names in your notes. Keep it to about 25 minutes. If he says WRAP IT UP, stop and write the addendum.

**What we already know (don't re-ask):**
- His career pivot: trained in construction and finance; a new tax law made development "a ghost town"; moved into financial planning.
- "No one could put a logo on my business card."
- His office: Balanced Financial, Fort Collins Tax Advisory Group, Balanced Wealth Partners.
- His clients: engineers and long-time employees, 60–68, $1M–$4M, wanting $200K–$300K a year.
- His process, the PATH Plan: Paycheck, Assets, Taxes, Heirs, walked again every year.
- Two stories: a 2021 couple repositioned before the 2022 drop; a couple whose trust and POAs were finished one year and one day before the husband's sudden death.
- His phrases: "a path to run on," "head trash," "two plus two equals four," "tenants and toilets," "lemonade out of lemons."
- Faith stays out of the business. Do not ask about it.

**Gaps to fill, in this order (12 at most):**
1. **Your own family:** has planning, or the lack of it, ever hit your own family (parents, in-laws, a friend)? Who, what happened, what it cost, what you learned. Scene-level detail.
2. **The kitchen-table explanations:** how do you explain (a) sequence-of-returns risk, (b) why a Roth conversion can make sense, (c) why a will doesn't keep you out of court, to an engineer across the table? Your best analogies.
3. **The excuses:** the three things good savers say to put this off ("I'm an engineer, I'll do it myself," "my CPA handles taxes," "we'll do the estate stuff later"). What's underneath each, and how do you answer?
4. **Paycheck:** a client whose paycheck plan changed their retirement date or their peace of mind. What did the map show?
5. **Social Security:** a couple where the claiming decision for the survivor mattered.
6. **Assets / long-term care:** a story where long-term care was planned, or wasn't.
7. **Taxes:** a client whose tax return, in your CPA What-If review, revealed something big (a conversion window, an IRMAA surprise, RSUs).
8. **Tenants and toilets:** your own landlord stories, and one client who sold rentals to fund retirement. Before and after.
9. **The business:** what you learned buying and selling businesses, and one client business-sale story.
10. **A family that waited:** someone who came in too late. What happened?
11. **What good looks like:** a family that had everything in place before anything went wrong. What did that look like on the hard day?
12. **The resistant engineer:** a client who doubted the whole idea and changed his mind. What moved him? And: what should a reader do if they never call you? (How to choose any advisor.)

Then write a **Book Interview Addendum**: each answer summarized with exact quotes, every story in Problem → Insight → Action → Result form, a list of people mentioned (for consent), and any numbers to verify.
