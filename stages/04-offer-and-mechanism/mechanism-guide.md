# How to Build and Name a Unique Mechanism

The Unique Mechanism is the **named, visual, step-by-step process** that explains *why* this advisor gets results other approaches don't. It is the most reused asset in the system:

- **Website:** the hero section and the "How We Work" page
- **Webinars:** the structure of every webinar (each one teaches one step in depth)
- **Assessments:** they score the prospect on each step
- **Appointments:** the meeting agenda *is* the mechanism, so the sales process and the marketing match
- **Book:** the chapter architecture
- **Reviews:** annual client reviews walk through the steps again, which drives retention and referrals

A prospect who can repeat the mechanism's name and draw its steps after a webinar has already half-decided to book.

---

## Rule 0: It must be real

The mechanism is built from **what the advisor actually does** (Knowledge Base section 5, "Process"). We name, order, and package it. We don't invent a process the advisor won't follow in the meeting. If the advisor's process has a gap (e.g., they never discuss taxes), either the advisor adopts that step for real, or it stays out.

Anything we add that the advisor doesn't do yet is labeled `[NEW STEP — ADVISOR MUST ADOPT]` and confirmed with them.

---

## Part 1: Two mechanisms, problem and solution

Strong positioning names **both**:

### A. The Problem Mechanism: *why things haven't worked*
The hidden reason the prospect is stuck, which isn't their fault and isn't what they assumed.

> Formula: "The real reason [ideal client] [bad outcome] isn't [what they blame]. It's [hidden cause]."

- It must be **true** (supported by the research in Stage 03 or by the advisor's lived experience) and **new to the prospect**.
- It shifts blame away from the prospect ("you didn't do anything wrong") and away from obvious villains toward a **structural cause** the advisor's process fixes.
- Give it a name when it's strong: *The Paycheck Gap, The Probate Trap, The Tax Time Bomb, The Care Cliff, The Single-Point-of-Failure Plan.*

Fictional example:
> "The real reason good savers still feel anxious about retirement isn't that they haven't saved enough. It's that they planned for a **number** instead of a **paycheck**. We call it the *Paycheck Gap*."

### B. The Solution Mechanism: *the named process*
The advisor's steps, packaged so they're memorable, visual, and clearly connected to fixing the problem mechanism.

---

## Part 2: Master mechanism architecture

Anywhere Advisor advisors run up to five webinar tracks (Estate, Tax, Retirement Paycheck, Asset Protection, LTC). Default to **one master mechanism** whose steps map to those tracks:

```
         ┌──────────── The [Name] (master mechanism) ────────────┐
Step:      1 Income      2 Tax        3 Protect       4 Legacy      5 Review
Webinar:   Retirement    Tax          Asset Prot. +   Estate        —
           Paycheck                   LTC
                 └────── every webinar ends with the same appointment ──────┘
```

Why it works:
- **One brand, many doors.** Every webinar is a deep dive into one step and points back to the full plan.
- **Built-in re-engagement.** Someone who attends one webinar but doesn't book gets invited to the next track. It's a different topic, but the same mechanism and the same appointment.
- **Cross-sell is natural.** A client who came in for income planning sees the remaining steps on the diagram.

Use a **single-track mechanism** instead when the advisor is a true specialist (e.g., only LTC planning). Then its steps cover that one topic.

---

## Part 3: Designing the steps

- **3–7 steps; 4–5 is ideal.** Fewer feels thin; more can't be remembered.
- **Steps follow the order of the advisor's real meetings and planning.**
- Name every step in **parallel grammatical form**: all verbs ("Map, Protect, Reduce, Pass On") or all nouns ("Income Map, Tax Plan, Protection Plan, Legacy Plan").
- Each step has:
  - **What happens** (one sentence)
  - **The question it answers for the client** ("Will my income last?")
  - **The deliverable**, something tangible (e.g., "Retirement Paycheck Map," "Tax Timeline," "Family Legacy Binder")
  - **Its webinar track and assessment section**
- **The deliverables make the process feel proprietary.** Named documents that the prospect can picture receiving are more persuasive than describing a service.

## Part 4: Naming

### Generate 10–15 candidates using at least 4 of these formulas

| Formula | Pattern | Example (fictional) | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome + framework noun** | The [Outcome] [Blueprint/Plan/Method/System/Roadmap/Formula] | The Retirement Paycheck Blueprint | Generic if the outcome is vague ("Financial Success Plan") |
| **Number + outcome** | The [N]-Step [Outcome] [Noun] | The 4-Step Legacy Plan | The number has to match the steps forever |
| **Metaphor** | The [Image] | The Income Bridge · The Family Fortress · The Retirement Runway | The metaphor must be easy to diagram and fit the advisor's voice (e.g., a former builder → "Foundation") |
| **Advisor-branded** | The [Advisor/Firm] [Noun] | The Ruiz Retirement Method | Only for advisors with strong personal brands; harder to sell later |
| **Acronym** | The [WORD] Method | The CARE Plan (Costs, Assets, Relatives, Estate) | Only if the word is natural *and* the letters map to real steps. Forced acronyms sound gimmicky. |
| **Problem-flip** | The [opposite of the problem mechanism] | Paycheck Gap → The Paycheck-for-Life Plan | "for life" may imply a guarantee; check compliance |
| **Niche-specific** | The [Niche] [Outcome] [Noun] | The Educator's Retirement Roadmap | Narrows the market on purpose; great with a strong niche |

### Score each candidate (1–5 per criterion; shortlist the top 3)

| Criterion | 5 = |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | A prospect knows what it's for from the name alone |
| **Memorability** | They can repeat it after hearing it once |
| **Ownability** | No competing advisor, trademark, or popular book uses it (see the checks below) |
| **Voice fit** | It sounds like something the advisor would say (Knowledge Base section 10) |
| **Visual** | It suggests a picture or diagram |
| **Extensibility** | It works as a book title or subtitle, a webinar name, and an assessment name ("Your Paycheck Blueprint Score") |
| **Compliance-safe** | No implied guarantee or outcome promise (see below) |

Compliance-safe is pass/fail: a candidate that fails is dropped regardless of score.

### Compliance-safe naming
**Avoid in names:** *guaranteed, safe, secure (as a promise), risk-free, tax-free, worry-free, never run out, protected (as a promise), wealth-building, beat the market,* and anything implying an outcome. **Avoid titles** like "Senior Specialist" or "Retirement Expert" unless they're an earned, accredited designation; regulators scrutinize senior-sounding titles. Process words (*plan, map, blueprint, review, roadmap, method*) are safe. The advisor's compliance approver signs off on the final name.

### Ownability checks (for the top 3)
1. Exact-phrase web search: is any financial firm using it?
2. USPTO trademark search (tmsearch.uspto.gov): any live mark in financial services (class 36) or education (class 41)?
3. Amazon: any book with that title?
4. Domain: is `[name].com` or a close variant available? (The GoDaddy connector can check.)
5. Social handles (optional)

Record the results. ™ may be used informally once the advisor and their compliance approver OK it. Registering the trademark (®) is optional and is a decision for the advisor's attorney.

---

## Part 5: The one-liner

Every mechanism needs a one-sentence explanation the advisor can say at a party:

> "I help [specific who] [achieve specific outcome] using [Mechanism Name], so they don't [the problem mechanism / the feared outcome]."

Fictional: *"I help teachers who are five years from retirement turn their pension and savings into a dependable monthly paycheck using the Retirement Paycheck Blueprint, so they're not guessing whether the money will last."*

---

## Part 6: The visual

Every mechanism gets a diagram (`python3 tools/mechanism_diagram.py`) in the advisor's brand colors:
- **Row layout** for website heroes and slides
- **Stack layout** for mobile, book pages, and worksheets

The diagram shows the name, the numbered steps, and (optionally) each step's deliverable. Keep step labels to 1–3 words.

---

## Quality bar: the mechanism is ready when
- [ ] The advisor reads it and says "that's what I do," not "that sounds good"
- [ ] A prospect could explain it to their spouse after a 45-minute webinar
- [ ] Every step has a tangible, named deliverable
- [ ] Every webinar track and assessment maps to a step
- [ ] The problem mechanism is supported by a fact ID or the advisor's own experience
- [ ] The name passed the compliance and ownability checks
- [ ] The first meeting's agenda follows the steps
