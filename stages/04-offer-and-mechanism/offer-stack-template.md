## The appointment offer (the most important offer)

**Never call it a "free consultation."** Name it after the mechanism, and promise a tangible deliverable the prospect keeps whether or not they become a client.

| Element | Fill in |
|---|---|
| **Name** | e.g., "Your Retirement Paycheck Blueprint Session" |
| **Who it's for** | Qualifiers (e.g., age, assets, within 10 years of retirement). Stating who it's *not* for raises perceived value. |
| **What they walk away with** | A named deliverable from mechanism step 1, e.g., "a one-page Retirement Paycheck Map showing your income, your gap, and your options" |
| **What happens in it** | 3 bullets that follow the mechanism steps |
| **Length & format** | e.g., 45 minutes, Zoom or in office |
| **Honest scarcity** | The advisor's real capacity (e.g., "I hold 8 Blueprint Sessions a month"). Must be true. |
| **Risk reversal** | "No cost, no obligation" (and mean it). **No outcome guarantees.** |
| **Pre-work** | Short questionnaire, plus the assessment if it wasn't taken yet. This raises the show rate and meeting quality. |
| **Call to action wording** | e.g., "Reserve My Blueprint Session" |

## Value equation check (for each offer)
Ask four questions: is the **dream outcome** clear? Is the **likelihood of success** believable (proof, process, credentials)? Does it feel **fast** to get value? Is it **low effort** for the prospect? Strengthen the weakest one.

## Compliance notes
- "Free" must be truly free, with no catch.
- If the advisor is an RIA and charges for planning, paid offers and the planning fee must match their Form ADV.
- Low-ticket educational products may not include individualized advice.
- Invitations that may lead to an insurance or investment discussion should say so where state rules require it.
- Scarcity and deadlines must be real.
