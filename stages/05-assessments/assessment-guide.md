# Assessment Design Guide

## The job of an assessment
1. **Make the prospect feel understood**: the questions reflect their real situation and worries
2. **Reveal gaps they hadn't considered**: each question quietly teaches what a good plan includes
3. **Sell the mechanism**: results are organized by its steps
4. **Qualify**: a few questions decide whether to offer the appointment or the webinar
5. **Hand off to the advisor**: the answers become the agenda for Meeting 1

## Structure
- **10–16 questions total, 3 minutes.** Completion drops above about 15.
- **2–3 scored questions per mechanism step.**
- **2–3 qualifier questions at the end** (timeline, asset range, sometimes marital status or state). Use ranges, never exact amounts. Always include "Prefer not to say," and count it as qualified.
- **Lead capture comes after the last question and before the results.** People who have invested 3 minutes finish the form.
- **Never ask for:** Social Security numbers, account numbers, exact balances, health details beyond "do you have a plan for long-term care," or anything a compliance department would treat as sensitive.

## Writing scored questions
- **Ask about behaviors and plans, not knowledge.** "Do you have a written plan for…" beats "Do you know the RMD age?" Nobody likes failing a quiz.
- **One idea per question.**
- **3 options, scored 3 / 1–2 / 0.** The best answer describes what a good plan looks like, so a prospect learns the standard just by reading it.
- **Plain English, 8th-grade reading level.** Define any term in the optional `help` line.
- **Make the honest "no" answer comfortable** ("Not yet," "Not really," "I'm not sure").
- **Order the questions by mechanism step**, so the experience mirrors the process.

## Score bands
Four bands work well: *Getting Started (0–39) · Building Momentum (40–69) · On Solid Ground (70–89) · Ready (90–100).* Name them in the mechanism's language.
- The **headline** validates the prospect ("that's exactly why you're here"), never shames them.
- The **message** points to the focus areas and the next step.
- Even high scorers get a reason to meet (the annual review, refinements).

## Step feedback
For each step, write `low`, `mid`, and `high` text: 1–2 sentences that name the risk or the opportunity in **the advisor's voice** (Knowledge Base §10), and tie it to the step's deliverable where natural. Educational, never a product recommendation.

## The call to action
- **Qualified:** the named appointment from Stage 04, its take-home deliverable, honest scarcity, "no cost, no obligation"
- **Not qualified:** the nurture offer (usually the webinar), plus a smaller "or book anyway" link. Never a dead end.

## Compliance
- A results note ("educational only; based solely on your answers; not a recommendation") plus the advisor's full disclosure in the footer
- SMS consent checkbox: **unchecked by default**, separate from the form, using TCPA-style language (the example includes it)
- A privacy policy link before going live
- No outcome promises in the band text ("you'll never run out" ❌)
- Submit the full question and result text for approval. The build tool flags risky words, but a person must review it.

## Track assessments (optional)
For a single webinar track, build a focused assessment (8–12 questions) whose "steps" are the sub-topics of that track, e.g., an **Estate Plan Gap Check** scoring Documents · Beneficiaries · Incapacity · Probate exposure · Family communication. Point its call to action at the same named appointment.
