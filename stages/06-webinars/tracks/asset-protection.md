## Problem block
**"Does this sound familiar?"** You've never reviewed how your home and accounts are titled · You don't have an umbrella policy, or don't know if you need one · You own a rental property in your own name · You worry about what a long-term care bill would do to your savings · You'd hate for your child's ex-spouse to end up with your money.

**The quiet worry:** "What if one thing we never saw coming takes what we spent forty years building?"

**It's not your fault (the enemy):** a fragmented system: the insurance agent, the attorney, the CPA, and the advisor each see one piece, and nobody looks at the whole picture of protection.

**From → to:** From "we've been careful" → protection layered on purpose · From assets in your own name by default → titled with intention · From hoping heirs are responsible → protections built into the inheritance · From scam calls that rattle you → a family plan for fraud.

## The three Big Ideas (defaults)
1. **"The five threats."** *(approach)* Lawsuits and liability · long-term care costs · scams and elder fraud · taxes · family events (heirs' divorce, debts, creditors). Self-scoring in the workbook.
2. **"The layers of protection."** *(themselves: "this is too complicated for people like us")* Insurance (liability and umbrella) · titling and beneficiaries · state exemptions (they vary; attorney) · trusts and entities (attorney) · fraud safeguards (trusted contacts, account alerts, a family "verify first" rule).
3. **"Protect the inheritance, not just the estate."** *(outside forces: "once I'm gone it's out of my hands")* Concepts like leaving assets in trust with protective provisions, instead of outright, so an heir's divorce or creditors can't reach them as easily (drafted by an attorney).

**Their way vs. our way:** Protection by accident vs. protection by design · Each professional sees one piece vs. coordinated review · Planning after a threat appears vs. planning before (when it's still possible) · Leave it outright to the kids vs. leave it with protections.

**Analogies:** layers of an onion, or the walls, roof, and locks of a house · a seatbelt: it only works if it's on before the accident · a castle with an open drawbridge (the unprotected inheritance).

**Objections:** "We're not rich enough to be sued." → Lawsuits target homeowners with insurance and savings. · "This sounds like hiding money." → No. Protection is legal planning done in advance and in the open; hiding assets is not, and we don't do it. · "Insurance covers us." → Coverage limits and exclusions matter; let's check them. · "This is a lawyer thing." → Yes, partly, and we coordinate with one.

**Why now:** before any claim or care need exists (planning after the fact may not be possible) · buying or retiring from a business · acquiring a rental property · a child marrying or divorcing · a recent scam attempt.

## Track compliance
- **Heavily legal.** All titling, trust, entity, exemption, and Medicaid content is `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` and must point to a licensed attorney. The advisor gives no legal advice.
- **Never suggest hiding or moving assets to defeat existing creditors.** Fraudulent-transfer laws apply; frame everything as planning in advance.
- State rules vary widely. Name the state on any state-specific slide.
- Insurance coverage specifics only if the advisor is licensed for that line.
