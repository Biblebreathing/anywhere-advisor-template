# Track Template: Estate Planning

**Mechanism step:** Legacy · **Appointment deliverable example:** a Family Legacy Summary (what you have, who gets it, what's missing)
**Who it's for:** adults 55+ with a home and savings, especially those with no plan, an old will, or an unfunded trust; adult children worried about their parents.

## Registration hook
**Title formulas** (pick and adapt 2–3):
- "The 5 Estate Planning Mistakes That Leave Families in Court, and How to Avoid Them" *(Bk S1 "N mistakes")*
- "Why a Will Isn't Enough: What Every Family Should Know Before It's Too Late"
- "How to Protect Your Family From Probate, Even If You Don't Think You Have an 'Estate'"
- "The [Mechanism] Legacy Class: A Free Workshop for [audience]"
- "Hope Is Not an Estate Plan" (a strong theme title)

**Three promises** (repeat word for word on slide 8):
1. What really happens to your home and savings if you die or can't make decisions, and how to keep your family out of court
2. The overlooked pieces that undo even good plans (beneficiaries, titling, an unfunded trust)
3. A simple checklist to know exactly where your plan stands today

**Call-out:** You're in the right place if you have an old will (or none) · you've meant to "get to it" for years · you have a trust but aren't sure it's set up right · you're worried about leaving a mess for your kids · you own property in more than one state.

## XYZ + M
- **X:** "We keep meaning to do this." Quiet worry about leaving a mess, family conflict, and the court deciding.
- **Y:** a clear, current plan; the family knows what to do; their wishes are in writing
- **Z:** peace of mind, protected relationships, and a legacy that means something, not a fight
- **M:** the advisor's mechanism, Legacy step

## Through Line options
- **Wrong:** "Estate planning is for rich people, and I have a will, so we're fine." → **True:** "A plan isn't about how much you have; it's about who decides when you can't, and keeping your family out of court."
- **Wrong:** "The deadline is death." → **True:** "The deadline is the day you can no longer make decisions." *(incapacity)*

## Problem block
**"Does this sound familiar?"**
- You've said "we really need to do our will" more than once this year
- Your will was signed before your kids were grown, or before you moved here
- You're not sure who your account beneficiaries are
- You have a trust, but you've never checked whether your accounts are in it
- You worry your kids would argue, or wouldn't know where anything is

**Arithmetic: facts to pull from the registry:** the share of adults without a will (a consumer survey) · the state's typical probate timeline and costs, and its small-estate limit · whether the state has an estate or inheritance tax · public-record status of probate · (optional) federal estate exemption, mainly to say most families *aren't* affected by federal estate tax, so the real issue is probate and control.

**The quiet worry:** "What happens to my spouse, or my kids, if I'm the one who handles everything and something happens to me?"

**It's not your fault (the enemy):** the do-it-yourself, one-document myth: a system that sells "get a will" as if it finishes the job, when the real failures happen in the pieces nobody explains (beneficiaries, titling, incapacity).

**From → to:** From "we'll get to it" → done this season · From guessing → knowing exactly what happens · From court deciding → you deciding · From "the kids will figure it out" → a family that knows the plan.

## The three Big Ideas (defaults)
1. **"A will doesn't keep you out of probate court."** *(breaks the approach belief)* Teach: what probate is; what passes **outside** it (beneficiary designations, joint ownership with survivorship, transfer/payable-on-death, assets titled in a funded trust); and why a will alone means the court supervises. Example: the anchor story or a probate story from the Knowledge Base.
2. **"Incapacity is the part almost everyone misses."** *(breaks the themselves belief: "I'm healthy, there's time")* Teach: a financial power of attorney and a health care directive; what happens without them (the family may need court-appointed guardianship or conservatorship). Example: a capacity story.
3. **"A plan nobody maintains is a plan that fails."** *(breaks the outside forces belief: "the documents will handle it")* Teach: funding a trust, updating beneficiaries after life events, organizing documents, and telling the family. Example: the "unfunded trust" or "outdated beneficiary" story.

**Their way vs. our way:** Signs a document and files it away vs. coordinates documents, titling, and beneficiaries · One meeting vs. a plan reviewed at life events · The family finds out at the funeral vs. the family knows the plan · Guessing what passes how vs. a one-page summary of what goes where.

**Analogies:** a map with no roads drawn in · the house built with no plumbing connected (the trust with nothing in it) · a fire drill your family has never practiced.

**Objections (with answer direction):**
- "I don't have enough to need this." → Probate and incapacity affect ordinary estates; small estates can still go through court.
- "My kids will figure it out." → Kindness is giving them instructions, not puzzles.
- "Lawyers are expensive." → Compare the cost of planning with the cost and delay of court. The advisor coordinates; the attorney drafts.
- "I already have a will." → Great start; check it against the three Big Ideas.

**Why now (triggers):** a recent birthday or retirement · a health scare in the family · a new grandchild · moving states · losing a parent · a law change (only if one is real and current, with a fact ID).

## Engagement
**Chat prompts:** "Type YES if you have a will." · "Type the year your will was signed, or NONE." · "Type ME if you're the one who handles the money in your house." · "Which Big Idea surprised you most: 1, 2, or 3?"
**Workbook:** p1 What I own and how it's titled · p2 Who my beneficiaries are · p3 Who decides if I can't (POA / health care) · p4 My Legacy Readiness Check (10 yes/no) · p5 Questions for my Legacy Session
**Seeded questions:** Do I need a trust or just a will? · What does "funding a trust" mean? · What if I own property in another state? · How often should I update my plan? · What does this cost, and who drafts the documents?

## Track compliance
- **The advisor is not the attorney.** Present concepts; legal documents are drafted by a licensed attorney. Never give legal advice or interpret a specific document. Name the partner attorney if there is one.
- "Not legal or tax advice" in Housekeeping and on the final slide.
- State probate facts are `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]`. Don't overstate ("a will guarantees probate" ❌ → "a will doesn't keep your family out of probate court" ✅).
- Family stories involving health, divorce, or money trouble need logged consent (Stage 02).
