# Track Template: Long-Term Care

**Mechanism step:** Protection · **Appointment deliverable example:** a Care Plan Summary (wishes, costs in your area, funding options to consider)
**Who it's for:** adults 55–75 (often couples) who are still healthy enough to plan; adult children who've watched a parent need care.

## Registration hook
**Title formulas:**
- "Who Will Take Care of You? The Long-Term Care Conversation Every Family Should Have"
- "How to Plan for Long-Term Care Without Draining Your Savings, or Your Kids"
- "Medicare Won't Pay for That: What Every Retiree Should Know About Long-Term Care"
- "The [Mechanism] Care Planning Class: A Free Workshop for [audience]"

**Three promises:**
1. What long-term care really is, what it costs where you live, and who actually pays
2. Why "Medicare will cover it" and "the kids will help" are the two most expensive assumptions
3. The four ways families plan for care, and why planning while you're healthy keeps more options open

**Call-out:** You're in the right place if you've watched a parent or friend need care · you've assumed Medicare would cover it · you don't want to become a burden to your kids · you're healthy now and want to keep your options open · you and your spouse have never talked about "what if."

## XYZ + M
- **X:** "We saw what happened to Mom. We don't want that for our kids, but we don't know what to do."
- **Y:** a care plan: wishes written down, costs understood, funding decided
- **Z:** dignity, choice, a protected spouse, and children who can be family, not full-time caregivers
- **M:** the advisor's mechanism, Protection step

## Through Line options
- **Wrong:** "Medicare will cover it, and it probably won't happen to us." → **True:** "Long-term care is likely enough to plan for, and Medicare covers very little of it."
- **Wrong:** "Long-term care is an insurance question." → **True:** "Long-term care is a family plan first; insurance is only one way to fund it."

## Problem block
**"Does this sound familiar?"** You've watched a parent need help with daily living · You're not sure what Medicare covers · You assume your kids or spouse would care for you · You looked at long-term care insurance once and it felt confusing or expensive · You've never written down where you'd want to receive care.

**Arithmetic: facts to pull from the registry:** the probability that someone turning 65 will need long-term care (HHS / LongTermCare.gov) · average duration of care · local costs for home care, assisted living, and nursing homes (CareScout Cost of Care) · what Medicare does and doesn't cover (CMS / Medicare.gov) · the state's Medicaid look-back and asset rules `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` · family caregiving statistics (AARP).

**The quiet worry:** "I don't want my kids to have to choose between their jobs and taking care of me."

**It's not your fault (the enemy):** the Medicare myth and the "we'll deal with it when it happens" culture, plus an industry that made long-term care sound like a single confusing insurance product instead of a family decision.

**From → to:** From "Medicare will cover it" → knowing who really pays · From hoping it won't happen → a plan if it does · From kids as default caregivers → kids as family · From one confusing option → four clear choices.

## The three Big Ideas (defaults)
1. **"Who really pays for care."** *(approach)* What long-term care is (help with daily activities); the limited role of Medicare; Medicaid after spend-down (state rules vary; attorney); and self-pay. Facts with fact IDs.
2. **"Care is a family event."** *(themselves: "it won't happen to me, and we'll manage")* The impact on the healthy spouse and adult children: time, health, careers, money. Story slot: the advisor's family story, if there is one (with consent).
3. **"Four ways families plan, and why timing matters."** *(outside forces: "the government or the insurance companies decide")* Self-fund · traditional long-term care insurance · hybrid life or annuity policies with long-term care benefits · Medicaid planning (with an elder law attorney), **plus** a written care plan (wishes, who decides). Insurance options require health underwriting, so planning while healthy keeps more options open. Concepts only.

**Their way vs. our way:** Assume Medicare pays vs. know the real funding sources · Wait for a crisis vs. plan while healthy · One product pitched vs. four options compared for your family · The kids find out in the emergency room vs. wishes written down and shared.

**Analogies:** a fire extinguisher bought after the fire is useless · the oxygen mask: put yours on first so you can help your family · a family meeting you hold now vs. one in a hospital hallway.

**Objections:** "It's too expensive." → There are several ways to plan, including no insurance at all; the cost of no plan is often higher. · "I'll self-insure." → That's a valid plan; let's make sure it's a plan, not a hope. · "I'll never go to a nursing home." → Most care happens at home, which is exactly why planning matters. · "I'm too young / too old." → Options change with age and health; that's the point of looking now.

**Why now:** health still allows underwriting · turning 60 or 65 · a parent's care experience · retirement (the loss of employer coverage options) · a spouse's diagnosis.

## Engagement
**Chat prompts:** "Type YES if you've helped care for a parent or loved one." · "True or false: Medicare pays for long-term nursing home care. Type T or F." · "Where would you want care: HOME, ASSISTED, or NOT SURE?"
**Workbook:** p1 My care wishes · p2 Costs where I live · p3 My Care Readiness Check · p4 My four options (pros and cons) · p5 Questions for my Care Planning Session
**Seeded questions:** What does Medicare actually cover? · Is long-term care insurance worth it? · What's a hybrid policy? · How does Medicaid work for care, and what's the look-back? · Can I still get coverage if I have health issues?

## Track compliance
- Specific insurance discussion requires the advisor to hold the right license. Keep it concept-level on slides; no product or carrier names.
- Medicaid planning is legal work: `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` and an elder law attorney referral.
- Probability and cost figures need fact IDs and are 🔁 (cost surveys update).
- No implied guarantees about qualifying for coverage or benefit amounts.
