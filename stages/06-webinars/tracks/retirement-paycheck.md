# Track Template: Retirement Paycheck

**Mechanism step:** Income · **Appointment deliverable example:** a Retirement Paycheck Map (income needed, dependable income, gap, options)
**Who it's for:** people within about 10 years of retirement, or newly retired, who have saved but don't have a written income plan.

## Registration hook
**Title formulas:**
- "Will Your Retirement Paycheck Last as Long as You Do?"
- "How to Turn Your Savings Into a Dependable Monthly Paycheck, Even If the Market Drops"
- "The 5 Retirement Income Mistakes That Can Cost You Years of Savings"
- "The [Mechanism]: A Free Retirement Paycheck Class for [audience]"

**Three promises:**
1. Why saving "a big number" isn't the same as having a retirement paycheck
2. The three risks that can shrink your income, and how planners address them
3. How to map your own monthly paycheck: what you need, what's covered, and the gap

**Call-out:** You're in the right place if you're within 10 years of retiring (or just retired) · you've saved but don't know how much you can safely spend · you worry about a market drop right after you retire · you haven't decided when to take Social Security · you want income you can count on.

## XYZ + M
- **X:** a pile of savings and a knot in the stomach: "How much can we actually spend?"
- **Y:** a written monthly paycheck plan: which income comes from where, and when
- **Z:** spending with confidence: the trips, the grandkids, helping family, without second-guessing
- **M:** the advisor's mechanism, Income step

## Through Line options
- **Wrong:** "If I've saved enough, I'll be fine." → **True:** "You don't need a number. You need a paycheck plan."
- **Wrong:** "The market will average out over time." → **True:** "In retirement, *when* the losses happen matters as much as how big they are."

## Problem block
**"Does this sound familiar?"** You check your balance more than you'd like to admit · You don't know how much you can withdraw each year · You're unsure when to claim Social Security · A market drop makes you want to stop spending · You've never seen your retirement income on one page.

**Arithmetic: facts to pull from the registry:** life expectancy at 65 (and the odds one spouse lives into their 90s) · Social Security full retirement age and the change in benefits for claiming earlier or later · recent inflation · a retirement confidence statistic (e.g., EBRI) · a sequence-of-returns illustration (labeled hypothetical, approved).

**The quiet worry:** "What if we outlive our money, and become a burden to our kids?"

**It's not your fault (the enemy):** the accumulation mindset: decades of being taught to grow a number, with nobody teaching how to turn it into a paycheck. *(the "Paycheck Gap" problem mechanism)*

**From → to:** From checking the balance with dread → knowing your monthly paycheck · From guessing what's safe to spend → a written plan · From fearing a crash → a plan for one · From "we'll figure out Social Security later" → a deliberate claiming decision.

## The three Big Ideas (defaults)
1. **"Your paycheck, not your pile."** *(approach)* Monthly needs vs. dependable income (Social Security, pensions, other dependable sources) = **the gap** that savings must fill. Workbook exercise. Example: the advisor's discovery ladder (from the Knowledge Base, if it exists).
2. **"The three paycheck risks."** *(themselves: "I'm careful, I'll be fine")* **Sequence of returns** (losses early in retirement), **longevity** (living longer than planned), **inflation** (rising costs). Example: two hypothetical retirees with the same average return but different order of returns (labeled hypothetical).
3. **"Building a paycheck that lasts."** *(outside forces: "the market decides my retirement")* Concepts: covering essential expenses with dependable income, a Social Security claiming strategy, and a withdrawal and "bucket" approach for the rest. Concepts, not products.

**Their way vs. our way:** Aim for a big number vs. plan a monthly paycheck · One withdrawal rule for everyone vs. a plan built on your needs and income · Hope the market cooperates vs. plan for a bad sequence · Claim Social Security when everyone else does vs. a deliberate claiming decision.

**Analogies:** a reservoir vs. a faucet (the pile vs. the paycheck) · a pension you build for yourself · a flight plan with fuel reserves, not just a full tank.

**Objections:** "I'll just withdraw 4% (or a rule I read)." → Rules of thumb ignore your real income sources and taxes. · "I'm not ready to retire yet." → The best planning happens before the date. · "I don't want to tie up my money." → Planning isn't the same as buying anything. · "My 401(k) provider gives me tools." → Tools project balances; a paycheck plan maps income.

**Why now:** within 5 years of retirement · a retirement date set · a recent market drop · approaching the Social Security decision · a pension election deadline · a job change or buyout offer.

## Engagement
**Chat prompts:** "Type your target retirement year, or RETIRED." · "Type YES if you've never seen your retirement income on one page." · "Which risk worries you most: SEQUENCE, LONGEVITY, or INFLATION?"
**Workbook:** p1 My monthly needs (essential vs. lifestyle) · p2 My dependable income · p3 My Paycheck Gap · p4 My Risk Check · p5 Questions for my Blueprint Session
**Seeded questions:** When should I take Social Security? · How much can I safely withdraw? · What happens if the market drops right after I retire? · Should I take my pension as a lump sum? · Do I need an annuity? *(answer at the concept level only)*

## Track compliance
- No income or return promises. Illustrations are hypothetical, labeled, and approved.
- "Guaranteed" income language refers only to contractual guarantees, with "subject to the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurer." Better: avoid it on slides.
- No product names. Concept-level discussion of annuities only, and only if the advisor is licensed to discuss them.
