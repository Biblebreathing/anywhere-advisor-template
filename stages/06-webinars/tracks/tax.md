# Track Template: Tax

**Mechanism step:** Tax · **Appointment deliverable example:** a Retirement Tax Timeline (year-by-year view of when taxes hit)
**Who it's for:** pre-retirees and retirees with meaningful pre-tax savings (IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s), especially those in the decade before required distributions.

## Registration hook
**Title formulas:**
- "The Retirement Tax Trap: Why Your Taxes May Not Go Down When You Retire"
- "How to Keep More of Your Retirement Savings Away From the IRS, Legally"
- "The 4 Tax Mistakes Retirees Make With Their IRAs and 401(k)s"
- "Your Retirement Tax Timeline: A Free Class for [audience]"

**Three promises:**
1. Why the "you'll be in a lower bracket in retirement" assumption can backfire
2. How required withdrawals, Social Security, and Medicare premiums can interact to raise your taxes
3. The planning moves to discuss with your advisor and CPA *before* the window closes

**Call-out:** You're in the right place if most of your savings are in a 401(k) or IRA · you're 55–75 · you've never had a multi-year tax plan · you've heard "Roth conversion" but don't know if it's for you · you worry about what taxes will look like for your surviving spouse.

## XYZ + M
- **X:** a large pre-tax balance and no tax plan; a vague sense that "the IRS is my silent partner"
- **Y:** a year-by-year tax timeline with deliberate decisions about withdrawals and conversions
- **Z:** more control, fewer surprises, more left for the things (and people) they care about
- **M:** the advisor's mechanism, Tax step

## Through Line options
- **Wrong:** "My taxes will go down in retirement, so I'll deal with them when I file." → **True:** "Retirement taxes are planned in decades, not filed in April."
- **Wrong:** "Tax planning is my CPA's job." → **True:** "Your CPA reports the past; a tax plan shapes the future, and the two should work together."

## Problem block
**"Does this sound familiar?"** Most of your money is in accounts you haven't paid tax on yet · You don't know what your required withdrawals will be · You've never compared paying some tax now vs. later · You're not sure how much of your Social Security will be taxed · You've wondered what happens to your spouse's taxes if you pass first.

**Arithmetic: facts to pull from the registry:** the current RMD starting age and the rule source · the current federal brackets and standard deduction (single vs. married filing jointly) · the thresholds at which Social Security benefits become taxable · the Medicare IRMAA income brackets and the 2-year look-back · the current QCD age and limit · any scheduled law changes, with dates.

**The quiet worry:** "What if we did everything right, saved for decades, and the biggest bill of our retirement is a tax bill we didn't see coming?"

**It's not your fault (the enemy):** the accumulation-only advice model: decades of "max out your pre-tax 401(k)" with no plan for how that money comes out.

**From → to:** From "we'll see at tax time" → a plan for the next 10 years · From one big pot → three tax buckets · From surprise tax bills → deliberate decisions · From the surviving spouse facing higher brackets → a plan for both of you.

## The three Big Ideas (defaults)
1. **"Not all dollars are equal: the three tax buckets."** *(approach)* Pre-tax, Roth, and taxable; how each is taxed coming out; why the mix matters more than the total. Example: an anonymized client "discovery."
2. **"The tax time bomb you can see coming."** *(themselves: "I'm not rich enough to worry about taxes")* Required withdrawals, Social Security taxation, and Medicare premium surcharges stacking up, plus the **surviving-spouse effect** (filing single after a spouse's death can mean higher brackets on similar income). Example: a widow/widower story, if one exists.
3. **"The window: the years when moves cost the least."** *(outside forces: "tax law decides, not me")* The years between retirement and required withdrawals; concepts to discuss: Roth conversions, withdrawal order, charitable strategies (QCDs), coordination with a CPA. Concepts only. Results depend on individual circumstances.

**Their way vs. our way:** Taxes figured each April vs. a multi-year tax timeline · Withdraw from whatever's convenient vs. a deliberate withdrawal order · The CPA and the advisor never talk vs. the CPA and the advisor coordinate · Ignore the surviving spouse vs. plan for both filing statuses.

**Analogies:** a business partner (the IRS) whose share you can negotiate in advance, but not after · paying for a seed vs. paying for the harvest (Roth vs. pre-tax) · a dam: release water on purpose, or wait for the flood.

**Objections:** "My CPA handles this." → Great. The plan gives your CPA something forward-looking to work with. · "Roth conversions mean paying taxes now — why would I?" → Sometimes it makes sense, sometimes not; that's what the analysis shows. · "Tax laws will change anyway." → That's exactly why flexibility (having buckets) matters. · "I'm in a low bracket already." → Then the window may be valuable.

**Why now:** the years before required withdrawals begin · a scheduled law change (with a fact ID) · retirement this year · the first year of Social Security or Medicare · a spouse's health change.

## Track compliance
- "Not tax advice; consult your tax professional" in Housekeeping and on the final slide. Encourage CPA coordination.
- **No promised savings** ("save $X in taxes" ❌). Hypothetical illustrations must be labeled hypothetical, use current figures (fact IDs), and be approved.
- "Tax-free" only with its qualifiers (e.g., qualified Roth distributions under current law). It's flagged by the build tool.
- Annual figures (brackets, IRMAA, the RMD age) are 🔁. Recheck before each airing in a new year.
