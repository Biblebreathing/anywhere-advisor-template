# The Anywhere Advisor Webinar Framework

One framework for every advisor webinar. It unifies Terrence McMahon's three bodies of work into a single build standard:

| Source | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **The 1-Hour Webinar Book** | The XYZ + Mechanism emotional journey, Pain → Paradise storytelling, the **PARADISE Atomic Offer**, and the **32-slide success deck** (Big Promise, Call-Out, Hell, End of the Line, It's Not Your Fault, Shift to Heaven, Hero's Journey, Big Ideas, Their Way vs. Our Way, Crossroads, Hire Me or Someone Like Me, This Is My Calling, Repeat Close) |
| **Webinar Warrior Prompt 03** | The **Through Line** (one belief, explained ten ways), the three-act structure, **story before credentials**, **three light booking callouts**, and the **word-for-word speaker script** standard (140 words/minute, 6,000–7,000 words, pauses, chat prompts, workbook cues), slide layouts, the camera-safe zone, and the quality loop |
| **The 400-40 Webinar System** | **Weekly cadence**, billboard slides, "teach three real things," **advocate, not closer**, seeded questions, run-of-show, and "every claim true without a footnote" |

It also folds in a widely used conversion idea: the **Perfect Webinar** framework popularized by Russell Brunson. The teaching section breaks three false beliefs (about the *approach*, about *themselves*, and about *outside forces*) so the audience talks themselves into the next step.

Benchmarks with sources (attendance, timing, length, reminders) are in `webinar-research.md`.

## 1. The foundation: four sentences before any slide

Write these first. If they're weak, no deck can save the webinar.

### A. XYZ + M (from the book, aligned with Stage 04)
- **X (current hell):** where the audience is now, in their words (Knowledge Base §3)
- **Y (paradise):** the transformation after working with the advisor
- **Z (what they can do now):** the life the transformation unlocks: time with grandkids, peace of mind, not burdening the kids
- **M (mechanism):** the advisor's approved Unique Mechanism (Stage 04). The webinar teaches **one step in depth** and shows the full diagram.

### B. The Through Line (from Prompt 03)
One belief shift that the whole hour is built to produce:
> **Wrong belief** (what the room believes now, reasonably) → **True belief** (what they'll conclude by minute 40)

Every slide in Act 2 must name which **angle** of the Through Line it serves. A slide that can't name one is cut. The ten angles:

| # | Angle | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong belief** | Says it out loud, and why it's reasonable to hold |
| 2 | **Consequence** | What the wrong belief costs |
| 3 | **Arithmetic** | The number that makes it real (from the fact registry) |
| 4 | **Story** | Someone it happened to (the advisor's or a client's, with consent) |
| 5 | **Comparison** | What most people do vs. what works (their way / our way) |
| 6 | **True belief** | Stated as a conclusion the room has already reached |
| 7 | **Method** | The advisor's named process, one slide per step |
| 8 | **Analogy** | A picture that makes it obvious |
| 9 | **Objection** | Answered before they raise it |
| 10 | **Trigger** | Why now and not next year |

### C. The three false beliefs (from the Perfect Webinar framework)
The three Big Ideas each break one:
1. **The approach:** "The way I'm handling this (a will, my savings number, my 401(k)) is good enough."
2. **Themselves:** "It's too late / I don't have enough / this is too complicated for me."
3. **Outside forces:** "The government, the market, Medicare, or my kids will take care of it."

### D. The offer: the PARADISE Atomic Offer (from the book), adapted for compliance
The offer is always the **named appointment** from Stage 04. Build it with PARADISE:

| | Element | For an advisor webinar |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Path from Pain to Paradise | The XYZ + M in one breath |
| **A** | Address objections | The 2 objection slides, plus Q&A |
| **R** | Risk reversal | "No cost, no obligation, and you keep the [deliverable] either way." **Never outcome guarantees.** |
| **A** | Affordable & valuable | Free, said plainly. The value is the deliverable. |
| **D** | Distinctive | The named session plus its named deliverable (the Paycheck Map, the Legacy Summary) |
| **I** | Immediate benefits | What they walk away with after 45 minutes |
| **S** | Scarcity & urgency | **Only true scarcity:** the advisor's real capacity ("I hold 8 sessions a month"), or a real trigger (a law change date, open enrollment) |
| **E** | Extra bonuses | Modest educational extras (a copy of the advisor's book, a checklist). Keep bonuses small: insurance anti-inducement rules in many states restrict gifts tied to a sale. |

## 2. The structure: three acts, 60 minutes, about 45 slides

**Shape:** about 45 minutes of teaching and story, about 15 minutes of invitation and questions. **Script:** 6,000–7,000 spoken words at about 140 words per minute.

In the table: **(Bk S#)** = slide number in the 1-Hour Webinar Book · **(P03)** = Webinar Warrior Prompt 03 · **(400-40)** = 400-40 system.

### ACT 1: THE ROOM (0:00–8:00)
Earn attention by naming *their* situation, not the advisor's résumé.

| # | Layout | Slide | Purpose | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | title | **Title + Big Promise** | The webinar title as a promise; advisor name and photo. Warm welcome while people arrive. | Bk S1 |
| 2 | bullets | **You're in the right place if…** | 4–5 call-outs in the audience's words, plus 1 "not for" line | Bk S2 |
| 3 | bullets | **Housekeeping** | Length, replay policy, chat, the workbook, the compliance line said plainly, and a hint that there's an invitation at the end | Bk S4, P03 |
| 4–5 | story | **The personal stake** | The advisor's own story (Knowledge Base anchor story). The real opening. | P03 |
| 6 | statement | **The turn** | The one line the hour pivots on. Large type, almost no words. | P03 |
| 7 | bullets | **Credentials, after the story** | Only usable credentials. "I share this not to impress you, but to impress upon you that I've sat where you're sitting." Name honest gaps. | Bk S10, P03 |
| 8 | cards | **Three promises** | What they'll leave with, matching the registration page **word for word**, plus the advisor's commitment ("I'll hold nothing back") | Bk S3/S5, P03 |

## 3. The speaker script standard (from Prompt 03)

The script is half the deliverable. **Write what the advisor says, not what they should cover.**

- **Spoken words in quotation marks. Stage directions in [square brackets]. A timing line at the end.** Nothing else.
- **About 140 words per minute:** 90 seconds ≈ 210 words · 2 minutes ≈ 280 · 3 minutes ≈ 420. **Total: 6,000–7,000 words** (revise if outside 5,500–7,500).
- Every slide's script carries: **the exact opening sentence**, **the transition into the next slide**, **pause instructions** ("[PAUSE. Count to three. It will feel like ten.]"), **chat prompts written out** ("Type YES in the chat if…" then "[Read two or three back by first name.]") **about every 5–7 minutes**, **workbook cues** (page and how long to stay silent), and the **three [BOOKING CALLOUT]s**: light, mentioned, moved on from.
- **Story slides get the most script and the least text on screen.** Write the full narration, then add one direction saying what to leave out.
- **It must sound like this advisor:** use the Knowledge Base §10 voice and their verbatim phrases.
- **Never invent texture in the advisor's life.** Every story detail comes from the Knowledge Base. No added kitchens, coffee cups, or weather.

## 4. Slide craft (from 400-40 and Prompt 03)

- **Billboards, not documents:** headline 8 words or fewer, 20 words or fewer of on-slide text, at most 4–5 bullets. **One idea per slide.**
- **Layouts:** title · statement · bullets · number · cards · table · story · process · offer · qr. Repeat them; don't invent new ones.
- **Type scale** (1920×1080): 120 / 72 / 44 / 32 / 24 px. **Nothing under 24 px.** (In PowerPoint points: 60 / 36 / 22 / 16 / 12.)
- **Camera-safe zone:** keep the **bottom-right quadrant clear** for the advisor's video. Non-negotiable.
- **Contrast:** at least 4.5:1 for body text (the brand palette from Stage 02 already passes).
- **Brand:** colors and fonts from `brand-palette.json`, and the mechanism diagram from Stage 04.

## 5. Engagement
- A chat prompt about every 5–7 minutes. Participation is what holds a room past minute 20.
- A **workbook** (4–6 pages): fill-ins that track the Big Ideas, plus a self-check that previews the appointment. Cue it in the script.
- **Polls** at the start ("What brought you here today?") and before the invitation ("Which step feels most urgent for you?"). Poll results become CRM tags.
- **Seeded questions:** 5 questions with prepared answers, for when the room is quiet. One public answer reassures everyone.

## 6. Cadence (from 400-40)
- **Start with ONE track:** the one the research summary (Stage 03) and the advisor's strengths support most. **Run it weekly for 6–8 weeks.** The talk gets sharp through repetition, not planning.
- **Replays run the weeks the advisor can't be live,** so the machine never stops.
- **Then add tracks in rotation** (e.g., week 1 Retirement Paycheck, week 2 Tax, week 3 Estate, week 4 LTC). Non-bookers are re-invited to the next track (Stage 04 re-engagement loop).
- The first several will be rough. Run them anyway. A finished rough webinar beats a perfect one that never airs.

## 7. Compliance (built in, not bolted on)
- **Education, not product promotion:** no product names, fund names, or specific recommendations on slides or in the script. Selling the *conversation* keeps the advisor clear of the rules that catch people who sell products from the stage.
- **No promises** of income, returns, savings, or tax outcomes. Every claim is true without a footnote, or it's backed by a fact ID **and** shown with its required disclosure.
- **The disclosure is said plainly in Housekeeping** and shown on the title and final slides. "Not tax or legal advice" for tax and estate topics.
- **Estate and asset protection:** the advisor is not the attorney. Point to a partner attorney, and never draft or interpret legal documents (unauthorized practice of law).
- **Testimonials and client stories:** anonymized, with consent logged (Stage 02); testimonials only if the firm allows them, with required disclosures.
- **Invitations:** where state insurance rules require it, disclose that insurance products may be discussed.
- **Scarcity must be true.** Bonuses stay modest.
- The **webinar is a marketing communication:** the whole deck and script go to the advisor's compliance approver before the first airing.

## 8. The quality loop (from Prompt 03)
Score 1–10 on: **(1) clarity of the Through Line, (2) value that stands alone, (3) voice match, (4) conversion: does an attendee who came to learn book without feeling sold to?, (5) compliance safety.** The gate is an average of 8.0 or higher with no dimension below 7, and compliance below 8 fails outright. Run up to three revisions; if the gate still isn't met, output "QUALITY GATE NOT MET" with the reasons.

**Five tests before scoring:**
1. **Read it cold:** read three random scripts aloud (including a story slide) exactly as written. If the advisor would have to improvise, rewrite.
2. **Word count:** 5,500–7,500 total.
3. **Angle:** every Act 2 slide names its angle.
4. **Value:** if nobody booked, was the hour still worth it?
5. **Voice:** same person as the Knowledge Base voice samples, or a marketer?
