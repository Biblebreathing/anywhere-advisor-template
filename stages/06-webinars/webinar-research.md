# Webinar Research: Sourced Benchmarks for Advisor Webinars (Pre-Retirees and Retirees, Ages 55–75)

Compiled: 19 October 2026. Every figure below is tied to a numbered row in the Fact Table (R1, R2, ...). Nothing here is estimated or rounded beyond what the source states.

**How to read the sources**
- **Vendor data.** Most webinar benchmarks come from platform vendors (ON24, Livestorm, Univid, Contrast, Goldcast, GoTo). Each one measures its own customers, mostly B2B, and defines "attendance" differently: some count live viewers only, some add replay viewers, and some report medians where others report means. **Do not average figures across vendors.** Compare a vendor only with itself over time.
- **Verification column.** "Read" means the source page or PDF was opened and the figure checked against it. "Excerpt" means the publisher's site blocked automated access (on24.com returned HTTP 403), so the figure was taken from the search-index excerpt of that official page and still needs a manual check. "Secondary" means the figure was confirmed only in third-party coverage of a primary report.
- **Dated.** Anything published before 2022 is marked **DATED**.
- **Age data.** No vendor report found breaks webinar behavior down by attendee age. All age-specific evidence here comes from Pew Research Center and AARP research on technology use in general, not on webinars.

## 1. Ten most useful findings for designing advisor webinars

1. **Plan for a little over half of registrants to show up live, and don't assume more.** Recent platform benchmarks for registrant-to-attendee rates are 60% (ON24, 2025 data, R1), 51.3% (Livestorm, 2025 data, R5), 49% live and 57% including replay (Univid, R6), and about 61% live plus on-demand (Contrast, R7). Goldcast's B2B average is much lower at 33% (R8). None of these is specific to advisors or consumers.
2. **Financial-services webinars hold attention for about 46 minutes.** ON24's 2025 Financial Services report gives an average of 185 attendees per session and 46 minutes of engagement (R10). Professional-services webinars averaged 56 minutes (R11).
3. **Most attendees do not stay for the whole hour.** Livestorm reports an average watch time of 26 minutes, "about 38% of a typical webinar's runtime" (R20). Goldcast reports about 29 minutes (R21). Contrast says engagement "typically begins to decline around minute 22" (R22). Univid finds 30-minute webinars get a higher share watched than 45–90 minute ones (R19).
4. **Mid-week works, and finance may be an evening exception.** The best-attended days are Tuesday through Thursday in Univid (R13), Contrast (R14), ON24 2020 (R16, DATED), and GoTo 2019 (R17, DATED). Goldcast is the only source with a finance-specific slot, and it found the finance peak was **Mondays, 6–9 PM** (R15). That evening slot fits retirees and pre-retirees who are still working, but it is industry data, not age data.
5. **Many people register late.** In Livestorm's 2025 data, 49.6% of registrations came in the final 7 days and 15% on the day of the event (R25). GoTo's older data shows a similar pattern: 59% in the prior week and 17% on the day (R26, DATED). Keep promoting until the start time.
6. **Reminder timing matters, but no rigorous webinar study measures the effect.** WebinarGeek's own data shows 46% attendance when the invite or reminder lands within 24 hours but more than an hour before, against 39% when it lands only an hour before (R29). No controlled data was found for "1 hour" or "15 minute" reminders (R31). For text messages, the strongest evidence comes from healthcare: a Cochrane review found SMS reminders raised appointment attendance (risk ratio 1.14; 67.8% to 78.6%) (R30).
7. **Replays are a large share of total viewing, so plan the replay as part of the funnel.** On-demand made up 43% of ON24 attendees in 2025 (R33), 42% of views on Contrast (R34), and 24% replay-only on Univid (R35). Livestorm found public replays got 368% more views than gated ones (R37).
8. **Interaction is linked to more action at the call to action (correlation, not proof).** On Univid, webinars with every engagement feature turned on converted 33% at the CTA, against a 22% average. Attendees with 5–10 reactions converted at 69% (R40). In a controlled lab study, short quizzes during an online lecture halved mind-wandering (about 40% down to about 20%) and improved retention (R44). That supports inserting polls or self-check questions every few minutes.
9. **No credible, independent webinar-to-appointment benchmark exists for advisors.** The figures that circulate come from lead-generation vendors and are unaudited (R49). The best independent data is Kitces Research's 2026 study: seminars cost **$1.76 per $1 of new annual revenue** and are used by 23% of practices (R47). Track your own registrant to attendee to booked meeting to client numbers.
10. **The audience is online, but older adults use video less often and are less confident with technology.** In 2025, 90% of adults 65+ used the internet, 78% owned a smartphone, and 64% used YouTube; for ages 50–64 the figures were 96%, 90% and 85% (R51–R53). In 2021, 64% of adults 65+ had ever made a video call, against 78% of 50–64 year-olds, and only 7% did so daily (R54). 54% of 65–74 year-olds had "lower tech readiness" (R55). Make joining simple: phone dial-in, test links, and a help contact.

**Compliance (applies to every finding above).** A scripted webinar is both a FINRA retail communication (if a broker-dealer is involved) and an SEC Marketing Rule advertisement. Recordings sent out afterward are also advertisements. Testimonials need specific disclosures. Regulators have specifically targeted "free lunch" seminars aimed at seniors. See R57–R64.

## 3. Practical implications for advisor webinars

Each item cites the rows it relies on. Where the evidence is thin or comes from a different context, that is stated.

**Attendance planning**
1. **Plan on roughly half of registrants attending live.** Recent live or overall show-up rates cluster around 49–61% (R1, R5, R6, R7), with a B2B low of 33% (R8). For a target of 40 live attendees, aim for about 80 registrants, which matches Contrast's own "80 registrations for 50 viewers" rule for combined live and on-demand viewing (R7). After 3–4 events, replace these with your own numbers.
2. **Keep the room small if you can.** Conversion falls as events grow: 43.3% for events over 100 attendees vs. 55.9% overall (R3, DATED). Univid's highest CTA conversion was at 50–100 attendees (R40). A local advisor's typical audience sits in that range.

**Scheduling**
3. **Test a weekday evening slot alongside a mid-week late-morning slot.** The general benchmarks favor Tuesday–Thursday and late morning or early afternoon (R13, R14, R16, R17). The only finance-specific evidence points to Monday 6–9 PM (R15). Pre-retirees aged 55–64 are often still working, and Pew finds 90% of 50–64 year-olds own smartphones (R52). Because no data exists by age (R18), run an A/B test: for example, Tuesday or Wednesday at 11 AM vs. Monday or Tuesday at 6:30 PM local time.
4. **Promote right up to the start.** About half of registrations arrive in the final week and 15–17% on the day (R25, R26). Keep ads and emails running on the day of the event, and allow same-day registration.

**Length and structure**
5. **Script a 60-minute event, but put the essentials and the first booking invitation in the first 20–25 minutes.** Sixty minutes is the most common format and has the highest total attendance rate (R19). Financial-services audiences average 46 minutes of engagement (R10). But the average live viewer watches 26–29 minutes (R20, R21), and engagement starts falling around minute 22 (R22). This supports the Stage 06 standard of a 60-minute webinar with three booking callouts, with the first callout early.
6. **Break the talk into segments with a check-in every few minutes.** In the lab, interpolated questions halved mind-wandering (R44). Live on-screen polls were linked to 15% higher completion (R41). Webinars with every engagement feature on had higher CTA conversion (33% vs. 22%, correlational, R40). This supports the framework's chat prompts every 5–7 minutes. Treat the size of the effect as unproven for retirees.
7. **Expect questions and plan for them.** Financial-services webinars average 15 questions (R10), and 92% of attendees expect live Q&A (R41). Prepare seeded questions and pre-approved answers (see item 12 on compliance).

**Reminders**
8. **Send a reminder within 24 hours of the start, not only one an hour before.** WebinarGeek's data is the only webinar-specific timing evidence, and it favors the "within 24 hours but more than 1 hour before" window (46%) over "1 hour only" (39%) (R29). Its methodology is undisclosed. Keep the 1-hour and "starting now" messages as convenience reminders, but do not claim a measured lift (R31).
9. **Add SMS for registrants who opt in.** The controlled evidence comes from healthcare appointments: SMS lifted attendance from 67.8% to 78.6% (RR 1.14), matching phone calls at lower cost (R30). That is a reasonable analogy for an older audience that leads with texting (R56), but it is not webinar data. SMS requires prior opt-in consent under telemarketing rules; this research did not review those rules.

**Replays**
10. **Treat the replay as a second webinar.** On-demand accounts for roughly 24–45% of viewing depending on the platform (R33, R34, R35). Goldcast's figure is much lower (R36). Send a replay link to no-shows with the same booking CTA. Public replays get far more views (R37), but gated replays capture leads. For acquisition, gating is the default; test an ungated replay for referral sharing. **Any replay sent out is an SEC advertisement and, under FINRA rules, a retail communication. Get it approved before sending (R60, R63).**

**Older-audience usability**
11. **Make joining easy for those least confident with technology.** About 1 in 10 adults 65+ do not use the internet, 30% have no home broadband (R51), and in 2021 only 64% of those 65+ had ever made a video call (R54). In the same survey, 54% of 65–74 year-olds reported lower tech readiness (R55). Offer a phone dial-in, a "how to join" page with screenshots, a link test the day before, a help phone number, and a browser-based platform with no download. YouTube reach of 64–85% (R53) suggests a YouTube or browser-based replay will be familiar.

**Measuring conversion**
12. **Set your own conversion targets and track them.** No independent advisor benchmark for webinar-to-appointment exists (R49). The best advisor-specific data shows seminars cost $1.76 per $1 of new annual revenue (R47, secondary), and earlier unverified Kitces data suggests webinars are more cost-efficient than seminars (R48). Track four numbers per event: registrants, live attendees, replay viewers, and booked appointments (plus clients won at 90 days). Treat a CTA click (22% average, R46) as a leading indicator, not a booked meeting.

**Compliance (applies to every webinar and replay)**
13. **Treat the script, deck, workbook, chat files and replay as approved advertising.** A scripted seminar is a retail communication needing principal pre-approval for broker-dealer reps (R58, R60, R61). Slides and handouts count as communications (R59). For RIAs, prepared remarks, slides and recordings are advertisements under the Marketing Rule (R63). The Stage 06 workflow of compliance submission before launch fits these rules. Keep the approved script and the recording as the record (R61, R63).
14. **Live chat is a communication too.** Chat answers to more than 25 retail investors, or files posted in chat, can become retail communications (R60). Use pre-approved chat answers and links, and route complex questions to the booked appointment.
15. **Use testimonials only with the required disclosures and agreements.** RIAs must disclose client status, compensation and conflicts, and need written agreements above the $1,000 de minimis threshold (R64). Broker-dealers must use the three FINRA testimonial disclosures (R62). If in doubt, use stories with written consent and no performance claims.
16. **Do not repeat the "free lunch" findings.** Regulators found every free-lunch seminar was a sales pitch, and half used misleading claims (R57). Label the event honestly as an educational session that includes an invitation to meet. Avoid exaggerated claims, and make any recommendation only in a suitability or best-interest process, not on the webinar.

## 4. Commonly quoted figures that could not be traced to a primary source

- **"Reminders 1 hour or 15 minutes before raise attendance by X%."** No primary study found (R31). The only webinar timing data is WebinarGeek's undisclosed dataset (R29).
- **"Up to 20 percentage points" from reminder sequences** (ZoomInfo) and **"6x attendance with SMS"** (SMS vendor): neither names a study (R31).
- **Thursday "64%" attendance and 58% attendance / 63% conversion from TwentyThree's "State of Webinars 2025."** These are cited by Zoom's blog (https://www.zoom.com/en/blog/webinar-statistics/, 16 Oct 2025) and roundups, but TwentyThree's original report was not located. They are left out of the table.
- **"29% register on the day of the webinar."** Seen only in third-party roundups with no primary source. The primary figures are 15% (Livestorm, R25) and 17% (GoTo, R26).
- **BigMarker figures ("11 a.m.–2 p.m. best," "45 minutes ideal," "44% prefer 45 minutes," "Wednesdays +14% attendance").** These appear on Zoom's blog and in roundups (Findstack), but no BigMarker report was located to confirm them. They are left out of the table.
- **Demio "22% attendance" for small hosts.** Only a secondary citation; the original Banzai/Demio source was not found.
- **Advisor seminar or webinar appointment rates (5–10%, 25%, 55–70%).** These come from vendors only (R49).
- **Kitces webinar CAC ($7,585) and efficiency (1.2).** The source PDF was not accessible (R48).
- **ON24 2025 and 2026 figures.** ON24's site blocks automated access, and its pages give conflicting values (49 vs. 51 minutes; 43%, 50% and 52% on-demand). Check against the downloadable report PDFs before publishing any ON24 number externally (R1, R2, R23, R33).
