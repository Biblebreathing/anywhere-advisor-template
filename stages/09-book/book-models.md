# The Three Book Models

Every advisor book does the same job: **make the reader feel the cost of waiting, make acting feel simple, and move them one step closer to the named appointment, without ever feeling like marketing.** The reader should finish thinking *"this person understands exactly why I haven't done this,"* not *"this person wants to sell me something."*

Pick one model per book. All three come from Terrence McMahon's own book work, and all three use the advisor's mechanism (Stage 04). Each one wraps the scaled Transformational Chapter Formula (`chapter-template.md`). The quality bar is Quill's KB-3 gold standard ("CALM Money").

**Shared specs:** 25,000–35,000 words · 3–4 parts · 12–15 chapters of 1,800–2,400 words (each chapter can be drafted in one sitting, One-Hour Book style) · 6×9 paperback · a QR code and a worksheet or reflection in every chapter.

---

## Model A: The Parable + Field Guide
*From the Marry Fenner book architecture ("The Estate Plan You Can't Afford to Delay").*

A **fictional family's story** carries the emotion; the advisor's **teaching sections** carry the lessons. The advisor appears as a stand-in ("the Advocate," "the Planner"), or narrates in first person.

**Chapter rhythm (never skip or merge):**
1. **Narrative Beat:** the family's story moves forward one scene
2. **What [Advisor] Wants You to Know:** the teaching, in the advisor's voice
3. **The One Thing:** a single action or reflection

**Structure:** Part I, The Wake-Up (what happens without a plan) → Part II, The [Mechanism] (one chapter per step, the family going through it) → Part III, The Legacy (life after, the side-by-side, the invitation) → Epilogue: a letter from the advisor.

**Cast:** protagonists who mirror the reader avatar; a cautionary family (the friend who waited); the advisor's stand-in; a professional partner (attorney or CPA) modeling the right division of labor; adult children who carry the family decisions.

**Best for:** Estate Planning and Long-Term Care (emotional, family-centered topics); advisors with few shareable client stories.

**Rules:** the copyright page says the characters are **fictional composites**. Never present fiction as a real client. The advisor's own real story still appears (introduction and epilogue).

## Model B: The Psychology + Myth-Buster
*From the FINISH estate book outline ("You Already Know You Need an Estate Plan… So Why Haven't You Done It Yet?").*

Built on the insight that **people don't fail for lack of information; they fail because they never finish.** The book explains why good people delay, dismantles the excuses one chapter at a time, reframes what's really being protected, and then walks the reader through the advisor's mechanism.

**Structure:**
- **Introduction:** "The conversation you keep having with yourself" (the kitchen-table scene where nothing happens again)
- **Part I, Why Good People Don't Do Important Things:** the most expensive item on your to-do list; present bias, procrastination, decision fatigue ("I'm not lazy, I'm human"); the silent weight of an unfinished promise
- **Part II, The Lies We Tell Ourselves:** one chapter per excuse, each titled with the sentence itself ("I Don't Have Enough Assets," "I Just Need More Time," "It's Too Complicated," "I'll Do It Later")
- **Part III, What You're Really Protecting:** decisions, not money; your family from confusion; the greatest gift is clarity
- **Part IV, Finally Finishing:** the old way vs. the new way; the [Mechanism] walked step by step; "Tonight you'll sleep better"
- **Epilogue:** return to the opening couple. This time they finish.

**Chapter feature:** every chapter ends with a one-page **"Finish This Thought"** reflection (e.g., "What has been your biggest reason for waiting?"). Reflection creates commitment without feeling like a sales exercise.

**Best for:** any track where inaction is the enemy: Estate, LTC, Tax, Retirement Paycheck. It's also strong for advisors whose interview surfaced a rich excuse catalog (Knowledge Base §3 objections).

## Model C: The Mechanism Field Guide
*The default when the advisor has a strong master mechanism spanning several tracks.*

The book **is** the mechanism. Each step gets a part or chapter cluster, so the book, webinars, assessment, and meetings all share one structure.

**Structure:**
- **Introduction:** the advisor's anchor story (Knowledge Base §2), the problem mechanism, and the book's promise
- **Part I, The [Problem Mechanism]:** what's really going wrong and why it isn't the reader's fault (2–3 chapters: wrong belief, cost, the true belief)
- **Part II, The [Mechanism Name]:** one chapter per step (or two for big steps). Each: a story → teaching (the Big Ideas from the matching Stage 06 webinar track) → worksheet → The One Thing → QR to the matching assessment section or webinar
- **Part III, Putting It Together:** a client case walked through every step (with consent); common mistakes; how to choose an advisor (including someone other than the author); the invitation
- **Epilogue:** why the advisor does this work ("This Is My Calling")

**Best for:** advisors with a multi-track practice and a strong process; the clearest link to the funnel.

## Weaving in the author's own story (all models)
The most persuasive material is usually the advisor's own experience, including the unfinished parts. (The Athan author knowledge base showed this: *"That gap is not a weakness in the author's platform. It is the book."*) Write **from the gap, not from the podium**: honesty about the advisor's own family and mistakes earns more trust than credentials. This needs consent for every family member involved (Stage 02 consent log).

## Choosing the model
| If… | Choose |
|---|---|
| The topic is emotional and family-centered; few shareable client stories | **A. Parable** |
| The interview revealed a rich excuse catalog; the reader "knows but hasn't acted" | **B. Psychology + Myth-Buster** |
| The advisor runs several tracks under one strong mechanism | **C. Mechanism Field Guide** |

## Title architecture (all models)
Give the advisor three options, drawn from **their own language** (Knowledge Base §7 quotes):
- **Primary:** promise + urgency without fear-mongering
- **Alternate A:** their strongest signature saying (often the best webinar title, so the book and webinar cross-promote)
- **Alternate B:** the softest, most giftable version (55–75 buyers often buy for siblings and adult children)

The subtitle states the reader and the transformation. Check the titles on Amazon (no close duplicates) and with compliance (no guarantees or promises).
