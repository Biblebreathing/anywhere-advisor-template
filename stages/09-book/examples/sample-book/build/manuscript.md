# Introduction: The Number and the Paycheck



Picture a couple at their kitchen table on a Sunday night. The statements are spread out in front of them. The number at the bottom is bigger than either of them ever expected to save.

And they are still worried.

She asks the question they have both been avoiding. "So how much can we actually spend?"

He doesn't know. Neither does she. They have spent thirty years building a number. Nobody ever showed them how to turn it into a paycheck.

That space between the number you saved and the paycheck you need has a name. I call it the Paycheck Gap. It is the reason good savers lie awake at night, and it is the reason this book exists.

Here is the promise. By the last page you will know what your retirement paycheck looks like, where each dollar of it comes from, and what to do about the gap. You will learn it through the five steps of the Retirement Paycheck Blueprint, the same process I walk every family through at my own kitchen table.

You don't need to become a financial expert. You need a plan you can see on one page.

Let's build it together.

# Chapter 1: Your Paycheck, Not Your Pile

For most of your working life, one number told you how you were doing. The balance went up, you felt good. It went down, you felt worse.

Retirement changes the question. The number still matters, but it stops being the point. What matters now is the paycheck. 

Here is the simplest way I know to see it. Write down what you need to live on each month. Then write down what will arrive each month no matter what the market does: Social Security, a pension, any other dependable income. The difference between those two numbers is your Paycheck Gap.

That gap is the job your savings have to do.

When people see it written down for the first time, something shifts. A vague worry becomes a specific number. A specific number can be planned for.
